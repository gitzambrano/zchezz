This file is the canonical short ledger for the current v5 NNUE investigation. Do not promote a candidate from a small screen alone.

## Root-probe teaching

The explicit child root-probe pipeline is operational and useful as an experimental teacher. It enumerates legal children, evaluates them cheaply, refines selected children with search, and produces policy/ranking labels without depending on MultiPV behavior.

The clean v5.06-rootprobe experiment produced three candidates from a 4,000-position mostly-on-policy corpus (~99.7k legal moves, ~7.47 refined moves/position):

- `b0`: policy/ranking only. Initial 64-game screen vs v5.06: 30W/19D/15L, 61.72%, +82.97 Elo, paired bootstrap CI95 [+27.20, +143.33].
- `bv10`: +small self-search value: 17W/24D/23L, 45.31%, -32.67 Elo.
- `b15`: +light Stockfish-19 reference: 14W/28D/22L, 43.75%, -43.66 Elo.

The large independent gate invalidated the `b0` screen:

- 400 games / 200 opening pairs, common seed with disjoint offsets 0/50/100/150.
- `b0` vs v5.06: **112W/159D/129L**, score **47.875%**, **-14.77 Elo**.
- Paired-bootstrap CI95: **[-40.13, +10.43] Elo**.
- Status: **NOT CONFIRMED / rejected as a promotion candidate**.

Conclusion: the original +83 Elo screen was a false positive. Root-probe remains a valid teacher mechanism, but this training objective/configuration did not produce a robust stronger NNU4.

## Layer-scope ablation

A shared v5.06 root-probe corpus was used to train L3-only, L2+L3, and all-layer variants. In all three cases the validation objective selected **best_epoch = 0**. Therefore no learned update beat the initial v5.06 weights according to the trainer's own objective.

48-game screens on identical openings were:

- control `b0`: 12W/19D/17L, -36.32 Elo.
- L3: 16W/20D/12L, +29.02 Elo.
- L2+L3: 17W/17D/14L, +21.74 Elo.
- all layers: 13W/24D/11L, +14.48 Elo.

Because the selected checkpoint was epoch 0, these positive short-screen numbers are not evidence of learned strength. They are further evidence that 48–64 game screens can fluctuate by several tens of Elo in this setup.

## Adaptive deep root-probe

Adaptive two-stage teaching used 4,000 positions, ~99.3k legal moves, ~10.69 searched moves/position, shallow 750-node probes and selective 8,000-node refinement.

The trainer again selected **best_epoch = 0**. Screen results:

- `b0` control vs v5.06: 14W/20D/14L = 50.0%, 0 Elo.
- adaptive vs v5.06: 11W/20D/17L = 43.75%, -43.66 Elo.
- adaptive vs `b0`: 15W/16D/17L = 47.92%, -14.48 Elo.

Status: rejected. Spending more teacher search did not solve the objective/search-coupling problem.

## NNU5 / v5.21 diagnostic — 2026-09-16

The frozen v5.09 NNU5 weights were rematerialized on the **v5.06 search code** as diagnostic `v5.21`, isolating the network from later search changes.

Architecture:

- HalfKP-8Bucket.
- 5120 -> 192 per perspective, concat 384 -> 32 -> 1.
- 1,979,344-byte weight file.
- Runtime/training feature parity had previously passed 16,110 cases; current code audit also confirmed the 8-bucket mapping.

The original NNU5 training was not data-starved or phase-unbalanced:

- 2.4M positions total.
- 600k opening / 1.2M middlegame / 600k endgame.
- 480,231 policy parents and 1,861,688 policy moves.
- Training/validation objective improved through epoch 10, which was selected as best.

Independent deep-teacher diagnostics used 1,200 fresh phase-balanced positions and Stockfish at 32,768 nodes. At 1,024 Zchezz nodes:

- `v5.21`: MAE 760.19 cp, RMSE 1310.16, teacher top-1 agreement 40.17%, near-even MAE 129.99 cp.
- `v5.06`: MAE 759.59 cp, RMSE 1273.39, top-1 38.25%, near-even MAE 125.17 cp.
- `v3.28`: MAE 750.09 cp, RMSE 1263.73, top-1 39.25%, near-even MAE 95.34 cp.

The larger NNU5 therefore improves one local ranking metric (teacher top-1 agreement) but does **not** improve overall score calibration. Its near-even calibration is worse, especially in middlegame/endgame.

Most importantly, fixed-node H2H removes throughput as the explanation:

- `v5.21` vs `v5.06`, 40 games at **200,000 nodes/move**: **15W/2D/23L**, score 40.0%, **-70.44 Elo**.
- `v5.21` vs `v3.28`, 40 games at **200,000 nodes/move**: **12W/1D/27L**, score 31.25%, **-136.97 Elo**.

Status: **NNU5 capacity scaling rejected as a strength direction in its present training formulation.** The old v5.09 loss was not mainly caused by NPS. The larger evaluator itself is less useful to alpha-beta search even at equal node budget.

Interpretation: offline BCE/value convergence, more parameters, more data and slightly better teacher-move agreement are insufficient. The dominant unresolved problem is **score calibration / evaluator-search coupling / training objective**.

## v5.22 static-evaluation calibration — 2026-09-16

A search-free diagnostic compared raw Zchezz `eval` scores with pinned Stockfish 19 at 16,384 nodes on 480 independent v5.06 self-play positions, stratified into 160 low-, 160 mid- and 160 high-material positions.

Overall:

- `v5.06`: MAE **162.12 cp**, RMSE **273.51**, Pearson **0.8246**, near-even MAE **87.23 cp**, sign accuracy 90.53%.
- `v3.28`: MAE **141.21 cp**, RMSE **249.05**, Pearson **0.8779**, near-even MAE **63.03 cp**, sign accuracy 87.88%.

Both engines compress score amplitude relative to the deeper teacher, but v3.28 preserves substantially better static structure/correlation. Fitting a simple affine teacher-from-eval map does not erase the gap:

- v5.06 fitted slope 1.359, intercept +18.3 cp, calibrated RMSE 254.91.
- v3.28 fitted slope 1.457, intercept -11.6 cp, calibrated RMSE 215.82.

v5.06 phase/material slopes vary strongly: ~1.53 low material, 1.29 mid material and 1.07 high material. Mid-material near-even error is especially high (~112.7 cp).

Conclusion: **v5.06's problem is not merely a single output-scale constant.** v3.28 is statically closer to a deep teacher despite similar global score compression. This increases the plausibility that v3.28's global/relational information contributes useful structure, while still not proving causal Elo for those features.

## v5.23 QAT vs integer-runtime parity — 2026-09-16

Code audit found that historical NNU4/NNU5 QAT and the C runtime do not use the same discrete activation equations. Training uses rounded normalized fake-quantized activations; runtime uses integer shifts (`>> 8`). A direct 3,000-position diagnostic with the **actual shipped v5.06 weights** quantified the mismatch:

- L1 activation-code mismatch: **32.45%** (93,460 / 288,000 codes).
- L2 activation-code mismatch: **34.54%** (20,725 / 60,000 codes).
- QAT-vs-runtime score MAE: **18.97 cp**.
- RMSE: **23.36 cp**.
- mean QAT-runtime bias: **+9.85 cp**.
- p95 absolute difference: **44.45 cp**.
- p99 absolute difference: **53.28 cp**.
- maximum observed difference: **67.34 cp**.
- **94.2%** of positions differ by >1 cp; **81.97%** by >5 cp; **66.5%** by >10 cp.
- only **4.2%** match after rounding to integer cp.
- score-sign mismatch: 1.37%.

This mismatch also exists in v5.06, so it cannot by itself explain why NNU5 loses to v5.06. However, it proves that historical offline validation/training optimized an evaluator that is materially different from the evaluator actually used by alpha-beta search.

Status: **high-priority causal hypothesis**. The next experiment must change only the training forward to be integer-runtime exact and retest the otherwise identical root-probe `b0` procedure.

## Established conclusions

1. Do not use a 48–64 game screen as promotion evidence. It is only a triage signal.
2. Use paired openings and paired-bootstrap confidence intervals.
3. Robust gates should use one deterministic shuffled opening order with disjoint offsets across shards.
4. Lower static/teacher loss or stronger teacher labels do not imply higher engine Elo.
5. Wider NNU4 alone failed; stronger Stockfish value teaching alone failed.
6. NNU5 scaling to 5120->192x2->32 failed even at equal search nodes; throughput is not the primary explanation.
7. v3.28 has materially better raw static-eval agreement with a deep Stockfish teacher than v5.06 on the v5.22 sample; the difference is not explained by one affine score scale.
8. Historical NNU4/NNU5 QAT is **not integer-forward equivalent to runtime**; on v5.06 the mismatch is ~19 cp MAE and affects roughly one third of activation codes.
9. The main unresolved issue is evaluator/search coupling, useful representation and training/runtime consistency, not basic NNU4 throughput or raw parameter count.
10. v3.28's extra global/relational feature channel remains architecturally interesting, but destructive un-retrained ablations cannot be interpreted as standalone feature Elo.

## Next experiments, in order

1. Treat **v5.06 as the only valid v5 baseline** until a candidate passes a large gate.
2. Finish GLB2. If a variant gives a meaningful positive screen, gate it with at least 400 games / 200 distinct pairs and paired-bootstrap CI, then replicate on an independent opening partition.
3. Finish v5.24 runtime-exact QAT. If its 96-game screen is positive and a non-zero epoch is selected, immediately run a 400-game disjoint-pair gate; if epoch 0 is selected, reject without chasing the short H2H.
4. If both GLB2 and exact-QAT b0 fail, combine neither blindly. Next use runtime-exact QAT with a **search-aware micro-correction objective** explicitly centered on deep-search deltas/child ordering and a very tight trust region around v5.06.
5. Never promote a v5 candidate based on comparison with the 3.x family; direct predecessor v5.06 is mandatory.
